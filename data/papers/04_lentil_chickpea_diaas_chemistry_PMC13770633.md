# Lentil, Chickpea, Microalgae, and Duckweed Proteins in Plant-Based Foods: A Food Chemistry Perspective on Composition, Processing, Functionality, and Digestibility

- **Citation:** Food Chemistry: X (2026)
- **PMCID:** PMC13770633
- **DOI:** `10.1016/j.fochx.2026.104744`
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13770633/

## Abstract
Growing demand for alternative protein ingredients has expanded research on lentil, chickpea, microalgae, and duckweed. Cross-source comparison remains difficult because material form, analytical method, processing history, digestibility model, and functional assay often differ among studies. Quantitative primary evidence was evaluated using a material-method-structure-function-matrix approach. Microalgal nitrogen-to-protein conversion factors ranged from 2.53 to 5.77, indicating limitations of a universal factor of 6.25. Processed lentil and chickpea showed fecal-protein-based DIAAS-type values of 44–58 and 78–84, respectively, whereas cooked chickpea showed true-ileal DIAAS values of 67–76. High-pressure homogenization increased lentil protein solubility while reducing particle size. Evidence remains broader for lentil and chickpea, whereas interpretation of microalgal and duckweed proteins is more condition-dependent. Direct validation in the intended food matrix is therefore more informative than cross-source ranking.
