# Beyond Nutrient Density: Linking Rapid Food Composition Assessment to Human Nutritional Adequacy and Bioavailability

- **Citation:** Frontiers in Nutrition (2026)
- **PMCID:** PMC13783529
- **DOI:** `10.3389/fnut.2026.2006560`
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13783529/

## Abstract
Nutrient density serves as a core criterion for identifying foods supportive of healthy and sustainable dietary patterns, yet concentration-based metrics offer limited insight into critical physiological processes governing nutrient utilization, including digestive release, intestinal absorption, and tissue-level bioavailability. This limitation has grown increasingly relevant amid the diversification of food systems toward plant-based alternatives, novel foods, and reformulated products, where measured chemical composition frequently diverges substantially from delivered nutritional value. This review frames rapid compositional assessment as an entry point rather than a definitive endpoint of nutritional evaluation, proposing an integrated continuum spanning measured composition, matrix characterization, in vitro digestion, bioaccessibility, bioavailability, and overall nutritional adequacy.
