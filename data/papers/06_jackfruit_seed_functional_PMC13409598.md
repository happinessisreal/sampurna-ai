# Effects of Brown Seed Coat Retention and Thermal Processing on Nutritional Composition, Bioactive Compounds, Antioxidant Activity, and Functional Properties of Jackfruit Seed Flour

- **Citation:** Foods (2026)
- **PMCID:** PMC13409598
- **DOI:** `10.3390/foods15142529`
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13409598/

## Abstract
Jackfruit seed is an underutilized by-product with potential as a functional ingredient. This study investigated how brown seed coat retention and thermal processing affect the color, nutritional composition, beta-glucan content, bioactive profiles, antioxidant activity, and functional properties of jackfruit seed flour. Seeds were processed as raw, boiled, or steamed samples, with or without the brown seed coat retained, using rice and wheat flours as references. Brown seed coat retention reduced lightness and increased redness. Jackfruit seed flour generally showed higher ash, protein, and fiber contents than reference flours, while seed coat retention further enhanced fiber content. Beta-glucan content ranged from 56.11 to 286.71 mg/g. Steaming better retained bioactive compounds, antioxidant activity, and beta-glucan content in brown seed coat-retained flour than boiling. Functional analysis showed higher water solubility index and water holding capacity (WHC) than reference flours.
